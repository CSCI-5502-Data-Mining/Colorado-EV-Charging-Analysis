A. Introduction

Why this problem matters

Colorado is rapidly expanding electric-vehicle infrastructure, but charger availability may not be distributed in proportion to current EV ownership, future demand, or community need. Residents who rent, live in multifamily housing, or live in lower-density rural areas may have fewer opportunities to charge at home and may depend more heavily on public infrastructure.

Our project studies how EV adoption, socioeconomic conditions, housing characteristics, and public charging infrastructure vary across Colorado. Rather than asking only where the most EVs are located, we want to identify places where charging access appears limited relative to current or potential demand.

Demand

Where does EV ownership suggest a need for more public charging capacity?

Equity

Which communities may face greater barriers to home charging or public charging access?

Geography

How do patterns differ between urban, suburban, and rural parts of Colorado?

Key stakeholders

  • Current and prospective EV owners
  • Lower-income and renter-heavy communities
  • Rural communities and small municipalities
  • Colorado state and local transportation agencies
  • Utilities, charging providers, and infrastructure planners

Project gap

Existing research shows that EV adoption and charging availability can differ by income, housing type, and geography. A charging strategy based only on current EV ownership could reinforce existing disparities. Our project narrows this issue to Colorado and combines demand, infrastructure, usage, and community characteristics in one analysis.

Semester blueprint

  1. Acquire and document Colorado EV, charging, usage, and socioeconomic data.
  2. Standardize the datasets to a common geographic level.
  3. Compare EV adoption with public charger availability and community characteristics.
  4. Identify areas that appear underserved relative to current or expected demand.
  5. Create maps and a transparent prioritization framework for future charging investment.

Background / Related Work

What prior work tells us

Charging infrastructure and EV adoption

Prior research has found that public charging availability is associated with EV adoption, while the strength of that relationship can differ across communities and income levels. This supports our decision to study charging access together with socioeconomic conditions rather than treating infrastructure as the only factor.

View source ↗

Equity in EV infrastructure

National research has documented geographic inequality in EV ownership and charging infrastructure by income and race/ethnicity. This motivates evaluating who is currently well served — not just how many chargers exist statewide.

View source ↗

Colorado transportation electrification planning

Colorado planning documents emphasize statewide charging buildout as well as access for rural, low-income, and disproportionately impacted communities. This provides direct policy context for our Colorado-focused analysis.

D. Proposal Overview

Scope, data, and constraints

Problem

Colorado's charging network may not align equally with EV ownership, future demand, or the needs of communities with limited access to home charging.

Goal

Identify socioeconomic and geographic factors associated with EV adoption and evaluate where additional public charging could best balance demand and access.

Major constraints

Demand proxy

Registration address does not show where a vehicle is actually charged.

Usage coverage

EValuateCO usage data cover only selected charging stations.

Temporal mismatch

Datasets may represent different years or update schedules.

Equity bias

Current EV ownership may already reflect income and demographic disparities.
